\noindent
This note documents the stochastic-thermodynamics estimators in
\texttt{src/models/jarzynski\_harvester.py}. The construction applies the
Jarzynski equality and the Crooks fluctuation theorem to an ensemble of
``work-like'' quantities derived from order-flow pressure, to recover an
equilibrium free-energy difference and to measure how far the flow is from
equilibrium.

\section*{Theorem (as proposed, with the honest reading)}

\begin{theorem}[Jarzynski / Crooks free-energy recovery]
Let a market state transition from regime $A$ to $B$ in finite time $\tau$,
driven by an external protocol $\lambda_t$ (order-flow imbalance), and let $W$ be
the stochastic work done along a path $\gamma$. Then the Jarzynski equality holds:
\begin{equation}
\langle e^{-\beta W} \rangle = e^{-\beta \Delta F},
\end{equation}
where $\Delta F$ is the free-energy difference and $\beta$ the inverse
temperature. By Crooks, $P_F(W)/P_R(-W) = e^{\beta(W-\Delta F)}$, and the average
dissipated work satisfies
\begin{equation}
\beta\,\langle W_{\mathrm{diss}}\rangle
= \beta\big(\langle W\rangle - \Delta F\big)
= D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(P_F \,\|\, P_R\right) \ge 0 .
\end{equation}
Individual trajectories with $W < \Delta F$ occur with strictly positive
probability (transient, Evans--Searles ``second-law violations''), but the
ensemble mean obeys the second law $\langle W\rangle \ge \Delta F$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Jarzynski equality is the path-integral identity for a system driven from an
initial equilibrium state; taking $-\tfrac1\beta\log$ of both sides yields the
free-energy estimator. Jensen's inequality
$\langle e^{-\beta W}\rangle \ge e^{-\beta\langle W\rangle}$ gives
$\Delta F \le \langle W\rangle$, i.e.\ $\langle W_{\mathrm{diss}}\rangle\ge 0$.
The identification of $\beta\langle W_{\mathrm{diss}}\rangle$ with the relative
entropy $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_F\|P_R)$ is the Kawai--Parrondo--Van den Broeck
relation, obtained by integrating the Crooks ratio against $P_F$. Existence of a
positive-measure set of trajectories with $W<\Delta F$ follows from the Crooks
ratio being finite and $P_R(-W)>0$ there. $\square$
\end{proof}

\section*{Implementation correspondence}

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{jarzynski\_free\_energy} returns
$\Delta F = -\tfrac1\beta\,\mathrm{logmeanexp}(-\beta W)$ via a numerically stable
log-mean-exp.
\item \texttt{average\_dissipated\_work} returns $\langle W\rangle - \Delta F$,
non-negative by the theorem (verified in the tests).
\item \texttt{work\_distribution\_kl\_divergence} estimates
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_F(W)\,\|\,P_R(-W))$ from shared histograms; it is non-negative
and vanishes when the forward and reflected-reverse distributions coincide.
\item \texttt{extract\_fluctuation\_arbitrage} reports $\Delta F$, the mean
dissipated work, the KL irreversibility scale, the fraction of transient
sub-$\Delta F$ trajectories, and the per-path functional
$\max(\Delta F - W,\,0)\ge 0$, routed through the transparent universal tithe.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Scope of the claim (honest framing)}

\begin{remark}
These are \emph{measurements}: a consistent estimate of an equilibrium
free-energy gap from non-equilibrium data, and scalar diagnostics of
irreversibility (distance from equilibrium). They are well-suited to regime and
microstructure analysis around high-volatility events.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
They do \textbf{not} constitute risk-free arbitrage. A transient trajectory with
$W < \Delta F$ is a finite-probability microscopic fluctuation, not a repeatable,
frictionless, executable profit; the ensemble still obeys
$\langle W\rangle \ge \Delta F$. The fluctuation functional is a magnitude, not a
P\&L: any strategy that attempts to monetise these fluctuations must be validated
against execution cost, latency and out-of-sample performance like any other
signal. We make no claim that the macroscopic second law of thermodynamics can be
monetised.
\end{remark}
